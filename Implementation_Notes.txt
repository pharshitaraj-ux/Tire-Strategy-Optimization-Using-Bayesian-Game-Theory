Bayesian Nash Equilibrium
#------------------------------------------
This implementation computes the expected payoff of each strategy 
using the observed weather probabilities. Under the assumption that each 
team chooses the strategy that maximises its expected payoff given the uncertainty in weather, the recommended
strategy for each team represents the Bayesian equilibrium outcome in your simplified model.
For a project, this is a reasonable and defensible formulation. 
In a more advanced research setting, one could extend it to model strategic 
interactions where each team's payoff depends not only on its own strategy but also on the strategies
chosen by the other teams.

OPONENT INTERACTION ANALYSIS(models/)-Why this is different from our previous Nash result

Previously we had:
E[Ui​(si​)]=P(Dry)Ui​(si​,Dry)+P(Wet)Ui​(si​,Wet)
So we determined:
"Given uncertainty about weather, which strategy gives the highest expected payoff?"
Now we're adding:
         E[Ui​(si​,sj​)]
So we're asking:

"Which strategy is best for Team i, given the strategy adopted by Team j?"

And the best response is:
BRi​(sj​)=argsi​max​E[Ui​(si​,sj​)]
This is the part that makes the strategic interaction explicit.

What we will analyze

we will vary: P(Wet)

he Bayesian expected payoff is:

Ui(s,Dry) = payoff under dry weather
Ui(s,Wet) = payoff under wet weather

Actual F1 tyre compound

and combined the compound with a stint-length category that we engineered:
Model strategy	Meaning
SOFT_Short	Soft tyre + short stint
SOFT_Medium	Soft tyre + medium-length stint
SOFT_Long	Soft tyre + long stint
MEDIUM_Short	Medium tyre + short stint
MEDIUM_Medium	Medium tyre + medium stint
MEDIUM_Long	Medium tyre + long stint
HARD_Short	Hard tyre + short stint
HARD_Medium	Hard tyre + medium stint
HARD_Long	Hard tyre + long stint
INTERMEDIATE_Short	Intermediate + short stint
etc.	

So Short, Medium, and Long are not tyre compounds. They represent the stint-duration category 
we created during feature engineering.
